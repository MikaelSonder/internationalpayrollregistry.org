Executive Summary

Payroll in Israel is the structured employer function that converts employment, compensation and attendance data into lawful salary outputs, employee deductions, employer contributions, authority reporting and payment-ready records. In professional practice, it is not merely the act of transferring salary; it is a recurring compliance process that connects HR inputs, income tax treatment, National Insurance, health tax, pension handling and internal controls.

The field matters because payroll errors can affect employees, tax and social insurance authorities, cash flow, accounting accuracy and employer credibility at the same time. Even routine salary processing usually requires coordinated review of compensation terms, variable pay elements, absence records, tax credit points, contribution status, pension treatment, deadlines, reporting formats and approval responsibilities.

In Israel, payroll operates through a monthly withholding and contribution cycle. Employers withhold income tax from employment remuneration, deduct employee National Insurance and health tax contributions, add the employer National Insurance share and manage mandatory pension and severance-related employer contributions for eligible employees. The employer reports and pays the monthly liabilities, commonly through the Form 102 reporting cycle, by the 15th of the following month. The annual payroll compliance layer includes Form 106, the annual wage and tax certificate provided to employees, and Form 126, the employee-level salary and deduction report submitted to the Israel Tax Authority and the National Insurance Institute under their respective requirements.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. Foreign employers with Israeli employees, local workdays, Israeli remuneration exposure or Israeli reporting touchpoints may need payroll review alongside tax, social security, employment, immigration and entity-setup analysis.

Purpose

The purpose of the payroll function is to ensure that remuneration in Israel is calculated, documented, reported and executed correctly, on time and in a way that supports employer compliance, employee clarity and operational reliability.

It exists to convert legal and contractual payroll obligations into repeatable monthly operations with clear controls, traceable outputs and predictable income tax, National Insurance, pension and annual reporting results.

Primary Outcome

Accurate and timely payroll execution in Israel, including correct salary outputs, compliant income tax withholding, appropriate National Insurance and health tax handling, mandatory pension and severance treatment, valid Form 102/Form 126 reporting, reliable supporting records and effective coordination with connected employer functions.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how payroll work usually progresses from raw input to completed payroll cycle. It matters because payroll is an operating sequence, not a single event.

1. Registration and SetupConfirm employer tax withholding file, National Insurance Institute registration, pension provider setup, employee onboarding and payroll configuration.
2. Data CollectionCollect employee master data, Israeli identity or passport details, income tax forms, tax credit data, salary inputs, pension fund data, time data, leave records and variable compensation items.
3. ValidationReview completeness, approvals, cut-off compliance, tax credit points, National Insurance status, pension eligibility and unusual changes.
4. Pay CalculationConvert inputs into gross pay, income tax withholding, employee National Insurance and health tax deductions, pension deductions, net pay and employer-side cost outputs.
5. Control ReviewCheck variances, exception items, contribution logic, sensitive changes and approval controls.
6. Reporting PreparationPrepare Form 102 tax and National Insurance payment data, pension remittance information and supporting payroll records.
7. Payment ExecutionRelease employee salary payments and complete related employer tax, National Insurance, health tax and pension payment actions as applicable.
8. Post-Payroll ReconciliationReconcile payroll outputs, Form 102 records, pension remittances, ledger interfaces and archive documentation; prepare Form 106 and Form 126 in the annual cycle.
Typical OutputsPayslips, payroll register, Form 102 reporting record, National Insurance payment record, pension remittance file, Form 106, Form 126, reconciliation support, correction logs and period archive.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the correct payroll route. It is presented as a logical workflow so that the reader can follow the sequence as an operational progression rather than as a table of detached items.

  1. Identify the pay event: salary, variable compensation, allowance, leave impact, termination pay, severance or adjustment.
  2. Confirm whether the employee or payee has an Israeli connection. If yes, continue to Israeli payroll review; if no, determine whether another jurisdiction or no payroll action is more appropriate.
  3. Check whether employer tax withholding, National Insurance and pension registrations and employee data are complete. If not, resolve setup before running payroll.
  4. Assess whether the employee is Israeli resident, a foreign resident or a foreign worker and whether tax, National Insurance, pension or treaty treatment requires parallel review.
  5. Assess whether the item includes a variable, exceptional or correction element. If yes, validate tax, contribution, pension, documentation and approval treatment before processing.
  6. Proceed to payroll execution, complete Form 102 and related payments and archive supporting records.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance explains why payroll in Israel cannot be understood only as a domestic salary process. International hiring, foreign worker employment, assignments and group structures often trigger parallel payroll questions across several disciplines.

RecognitionIsraeli payroll obligations may arise where remuneration, work location, employee presence, employer activity or a taxable employment connection is materially linked to Israel.
Foreign CompaniesForeign employers hiring in Israel often need local payroll mapping, income tax withholding file setup, National Insurance registration, pension review and operational setup before first payment.
Foreign EmployeesEmployers of foreign residents must report those workers and pay National Insurance contributions under the applicable National Insurance Institute rules. Employee tax and pension treatment must be separately assessed.
Applicable International RulesTax treaty questions, social security agreements, immigration requirements, assignment structures and wider international considerations may affect payroll treatment depending on the facts.
Language ConsiderationsDomestic payroll operations may require Hebrew-facing handling, payslips and authority communication, while international groups often run wider reporting and approvals in English.
Typical Cross-Border ScenarioForeign company hires first Israeli employee or foreign worker in Israel; group payroll model must localise for income tax, National Insurance, pension and Form 102 obligations; an assignment later requires treaty, immigration and coverage review.
Common RiskLate registration, incorrect tax credit or withholding assumptions, missing National Insurance or pension data, weak master data, poor coordination between HR and finance, or underestimating Israeli tax and social-security reporting touchpoints.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Must an Employer Run Payroll for Employees in Israel?Yes. Employers paying taxable remuneration connected to Israel generally need a compliant payroll process with income tax, National Insurance, health tax and pension handling.
Is Form 102 Important?Yes. Form 102 is the core recurring employer reporting and payment process for income tax withholding and National Insurance and health tax liabilities.
What Is Form 126?Form 126 is the annual employer report containing employee-level salary and deduction information for submission to the Tax Authority and National Insurance Institute under their applicable reporting schedules.
Can a Foreign Company Have Payroll Obligations in Israel?Yes. Israeli payroll obligations may arise depending on employee presence, work connection, remuneration flow and employer structure.
Does Payroll Include Mandatory Pension?Yes. Eligible employees are generally covered by mandatory pension and severance contribution requirements, which need to be integrated into payroll operations.
Is Documentation Important?Yes. Clear documentation supports income tax, National Insurance, pension accuracy, annual reporting, corrections, authority interaction and internal control quality.
