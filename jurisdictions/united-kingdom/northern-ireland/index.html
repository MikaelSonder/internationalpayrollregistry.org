Executive Summary

Payroll in Northern Ireland is the structured employer function that converts employment, compensation and attendance data into lawful salary outputs, employee deductions, employer contributions, HMRC reporting and payment-ready records. In professional practice, it is not merely the act of transferring salary; it is a recurring compliance process that connects HR inputs, PAYE, National Insurance, workplace pensions, statutory payments, Northern Ireland employment rights and internal controls.

The field matters because payroll errors can affect employees, HMRC, The Pensions Regulator, the Labour Relations Agency, cash flow, accounting accuracy and employer credibility at the same time. Even routine salary processing requires coordinated review of compensation terms, tax codes, National Insurance category letters, pension eligibility, variable pay, absence records, statutory payment eligibility, RTI submission deadlines, Northern Ireland holiday pay rules and approval responsibilities.

In Northern Ireland, employers generally operate PAYE as part of payroll. PAYE is HMRC's system for collecting Income Tax and National Insurance from employment. Employers submit a Full Payment Submission (FPS) to HMRC on or before each payday through payroll software. FPS reports pay and deductions for each employee. An Employer Payment Summary (EPS) is used to report adjustments to the employer's HMRC liability, including statutory payment recovery, Employment Allowance claims and no-payment periods. Workplace pension auto-enrolment adds a separate employee assessment, enrolment, contribution and record-keeping layer.

The key local distinction is that employment law is devolved in Northern Ireland. Payroll must therefore be coordinated with Northern Ireland-specific rules and guidance on employment rights, holiday pay, working time, statutory leave, redundancy, equality and industrial relations. This is especially significant for employers operating on both sides of the Irish border, where Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland have separate payroll, tax, social security and employment systems.

Purpose

The purpose of the payroll function is to ensure that remuneration in Northern Ireland is calculated, documented, reported and executed correctly, on time and in a way that supports employer compliance, employee clarity and operational reliability.

It exists to convert legal and contractual payroll obligations into repeatable pay-cycle operations with clear controls, traceable RTI outputs and predictable HMRC, pension, statutory payment and Northern Ireland employment law outcomes.

Accurate and timely payroll execution in Northern Ireland, including correct salary outputs, compliant PAYE and National Insurance deductions, valid RTI reporting, appropriate workplace pension and statutory payment handling, reliable supporting records and effective coordination with connected employer functions.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how payroll work usually progresses from raw input to completed pay cycle. It matters because payroll is an operating sequence, not a single event.

1. Registration and SetupConfirm HMRC PAYE registration, Accounts Office reference, RTI-enabled payroll software, pension provider setup, Northern Ireland employment policy inputs and employee onboarding readiness.
2. Data CollectionCollect employee master data, right-to-work and address information, starter declaration or P45 data, tax code, National Insurance number, pension details, salary inputs, time data, holiday, sickness and leave records and variable compensation items.
3. ValidationReview completeness, approvals, pay-period cut-off, tax code, NI category, pension status, statutory payment data, holiday pay inputs, student loan status and unusual changes.
4. Pay CalculationConvert inputs into gross pay, PAYE income tax, employee National Insurance, employee pension contributions, student loan deductions, net pay and employer National Insurance and pension cost outputs.
5. Control ReviewCheck variances, exception items, pension assessment, statutory payment and holiday pay treatment, sensitive changes, RTI readiness and approval controls.
6. RTI and Reporting PreparationPrepare and submit FPS on or before payday; prepare EPS where adjustments, statutory payment recovery, Employment Allowance or no-payment reporting applies.
7. Payment ExecutionRelease employee salary payments, provide itemised payslips, complete workplace pension contribution actions and make relevant HMRC payments.
8. Post-Payroll ReconciliationReconcile payroll outputs, FPS/EPS records, HMRC liability, pension remittances, ledger interfaces and archive documentation; complete year-end processes in the relevant cycle.

Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the correct payroll route. It is presented as a logical workflow so that the reader can follow the sequence as an operational progression rather than as a table of detached items.

  1. Identify the pay event: salary, bonus, overtime, allowance, holiday pay, sickness absence, statutory family payment, termination pay or adjustment.
  2. Confirm whether the employee has a UK employment and PAYE connection. If yes, continue to Northern Ireland payroll review; if no, determine whether another jurisdiction or no UK payroll action is more appropriate.
  3. Check whether HMRC PAYE scheme, RTI software, employee tax and National Insurance information, workplace pension setup and Northern Ireland employment policy records are complete. If not, resolve setup before running payroll.
  4. Assess the employee's tax code, National Insurance category, pension eligibility, student loan status, statutory payment eligibility, holiday pay position and any variable or exceptional pay elements before calculation.
  5. Determine whether an Irish-border or other cross-border factor exists. If yes, add parallel review of PAYE, National Insurance, Irish tax and social security, tax treaty, assignment, immigration or entity implications.
  6. Proceed to payroll execution, submit FPS on or before payday, make required payments, report EPS where relevant and archive supporting records.
Timeline

The timeline section provides a practical sense of how payroll work develops across recurring cycles and exceptional events.

Recurring Payroll CycleUsually driven by fixed pay-period cut-off, calculation, review, FPS submission, salary payment, pension contribution and HMRC payment timing.
FPS SubmissionAn FPS must generally be submitted to HMRC on or before the date employees are paid, whether employees are paid weekly, monthly or on another normal payroll date.
PAYE and NIC PaymentPAYE income tax and National Insurance liabilities are generally paid to HMRC by the 22nd of the following tax month when paying electronically, or by the 19th when paying by post. Eligible small employers may pay quarterly.
EPS SubmissionEPS is submitted when the employer needs to report adjustments, recoveries, allowances or no-pay periods. To reduce a monthly liability, it generally must be received by HMRC by the 19th following the tax month.
Workplace Pension ContributionsEmployer and employee pension contributions must be paid to the pension scheme by the applicable deadline, generally by the 22nd of the following month for electronic payment, subject to the scheme and contribution arrangement.
Tax Year-EndThe UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April. Year-end payroll includes final RTI reporting, employee P60s by 31 May and applicable employer benefit reporting or payrolling processes.
Statutory Sick PayFrom 6 April 2026, statutory sick pay is payable from the first full day of sickness absence for eligible employees, with the former three-day waiting period removed. Employers should validate current eligibility and rate rules for each payroll period.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance explains why payroll in Northern Ireland cannot be understood only as a domestic salary process. Foreign hiring, Irish-border commuting, employee arrivals, overseas work, assignments and group structures often trigger parallel payroll questions across several disciplines.

RecognitionUK payroll obligations may arise where remuneration, work location, employee presence, Northern Ireland residence, employer activity or a taxable employment connection is materially linked to the United Kingdom.
Foreign CompaniesForeign employers hiring employees who work in Northern Ireland often need PAYE, National Insurance, RTI, workplace pension and local employment law analysis and operational setup before first payment. The correct route depends on employer presence, payment arrangements, employee status and applicable agreements.
Republic of Ireland Employers and Frontier WorkersAn employer operating on both sides of the Irish border must treat Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland as separate payroll jurisdictions. Cross-border work can create UK PAYE, National Insurance, Irish PAYE, PRSI, social security coordination, tax treaty, employee residence and local employment-law questions.
Employees Arriving in Northern IrelandEmployers generally operate PAYE and National Insurance for employees coming to work in the UK from abroad, subject to applicable social security and HMRC arrangements. Right-to-work, employee address, tax treatment and cross-border social security must be handled accurately.
Employees Working AbroadPAYE and National Insurance treatment for Northern Ireland employees working abroad depends on work location, expected duration, employer circumstances and applicable social security agreement or coordination rules. Overseas work does not automatically remove UK payroll obligations.
Applicable International RulesTax treaties, UK domestic employment income rules, social security agreements, UK-EU Trade and Cooperation Agreement coordination, Common Travel Area arrangements, Irish domestic tax and social insurance rules, assignment structures and immigration requirements may affect payroll treatment depending on the facts.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Must an Employer Run Payroll for Employees in Northern Ireland?Yes. Employers paying taxable employment income connected to Northern Ireland generally need a compliant payroll process with PAYE, National Insurance, RTI and pension handling.
What Is a Full Payment Submission?An FPS is the main Real Time Information submission made to HMRC each time an employer pays employees. It must generally be submitted on or before payday.
What Is an Employer Payment Summary?An EPS is used to report adjustments to the employer's HMRC liability, including statutory payment recovery, Employment Allowance, no-payment periods and other relevant amounts.
Does Northern Ireland Have Different Employment Law?Yes. PAYE, National Insurance and RTI are principally UK-wide, but Northern Ireland has devolved employment law and distinct local rules and guidance may affect holiday pay, leave, working time, redundancy and employment rights.
Does Payroll Include Workplace Pensions?Yes. Employers generally assess employees for auto-enrolment, make applicable employer contributions, deduct employee contributions and maintain pension records.
Can a Foreign or Republic of Ireland Company Have Northern Ireland Payroll Obligations?Yes. UK and Northern Ireland payroll obligations may arise depending on employee presence, work connection, residence, remuneration flow, employer structure, PAYE, National Insurance, Irish payroll and social security agreement factors.
