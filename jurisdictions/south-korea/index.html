Executive Summary

Payroll in South Korea is the structured employer function that converts employment, compensation and attendance data into lawful salary outputs, employee deductions, employer contributions, authority reporting and payment-ready records. In professional practice, it is not merely the act of transferring salary; it is a recurring compliance process that connects HR inputs, income tax treatment, four major social insurance handling and internal controls.

The field matters because payroll errors can affect employees, authorities, cash flow, accounting accuracy and employer credibility at the same time. Even routine salary processing usually requires coordinated review of compensation terms, variable pay elements, absence records, insurance eligibility, tax treatment, deadlines, reporting formats and approval responsibilities.

In South Korea, payroll commonly operates through a monthly cycle. Employers calculate income tax withholding using the National Tax Service's simplified withholding tax table, withhold local income tax and calculate the employee and employer shares of the four major social insurances: National Pension, National Health Insurance, Employment Insurance and Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance. National Pension and National Health Insurance are generally shared between employer and employee; employment insurance involves employee and employer components; and industrial accident insurance is generally employer-funded with industry-sensitive rates. The employer also performs an annual year-end tax settlement for eligible employees.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. Foreign employers with South Korean employees, local workdays, South Korean remuneration exposure or South Korean reporting touchpoints may need payroll review alongside tax, social security, employment, immigration and entity-setup analysis.

Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the correct payroll route. It is presented as a logical workflow so that the reader can follow the sequence as an operational progression rather than as a table of detached items.

  1. Identify the pay event: salary, bonus, allowance, leave impact, severance, termination pay or adjustment.
  2. Confirm whether the employee or payee has a South Korean connection. If yes, continue to South Korean payroll review; if no, determine whether another jurisdiction or no payroll action is more appropriate.
  3. Check whether employer tax registration and four major social insurance registrations and employee enrollment are complete. If not, resolve setup before running payroll.
  4. Assess whether the employee's resident, non-resident or foreign-worker status affects tax or social insurance treatment, including whether an applicable social-security agreement applies.
  5. Assess whether the item includes a variable, exceptional or correction element. If yes, validate tax, insurance, documentation and approval treatment before processing.
  6. Proceed to payroll execution, complete authority payments and reporting and archive supporting records.
